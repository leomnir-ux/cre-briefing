# Weekly CRE Briefing Spec

## Purpose
A weekly reference for a student recruiting in CRE. I read the Market Summary every week, then pick the asset class sections I need (for example, only Industrial before a Prologis interview). Each section must make sense on its own without the others.

## Title
"U.S. CRE Weekly Briefing, [start date] to [end date]"

## Market Summary (1,800 to 2,300 words, not counting the Sources lines)
Write it as a flowing article, not a list of statistics. Structure:
- Six to seven topics, each starting with a ### headline that states the takeaway (for example, "Rates and the Fed: relief from Treasuries, none from the Fed"), not just a label like "Rates."
- Under each headline, one italic overview sentence that tells me what I am about to read.
- Then two or three flowing paragraphs of four to six sentences each, in plain language, with the details that make each story concrete (parties, size, location, terms). Explain what happened and why it matters, and tie facts together with connecting sentences instead of stacking numbers. No more than three figures per sentence.
- Then one "My read:" paragraph with the interpretation. Do not put interpretation anywhere else.
- Then a "Sources:" line listing the outlets with links for that topic.
Required topics: the week in brief, rates and the Fed, credit and CMBS, capital and pricing, and which asset classes are strengthening or weakening. Every figure must still appear in a linked source and match it exactly. This section must work as a standalone briefing.

## Capital Markets (about 1,000 words including the table)
Start with a "How capital markets are performing" overview of two or three paragraphs, then a table of indicators (what each measures, the figure, the source and its date). After the table, one caveat paragraph on any figure carried over or not re-verified, then short paragraphs headed Rates, Credit, and Transactions, each explaining what moved and why. End with two "My read:" paragraphs on what the measures mean together.

## Each asset class section (about 700 to 1,200 words each), in this order
1. "How [asset class] is performing:" two or three paragraphs, not a summary line. Cover vacancy, absorption, rents, pricing, supply, and any big news or updates, with the numbers that make the picture concrete.
2. "Capital markets read-through:" three to five sentences on how rates, spreads, lenders, and buyers are affecting this asset class.
3. "Market data:" the latest figures with the publish date and source link for each. Say plainly when data is older than this week.
4. Stories: 2 stories, up to 3 only if the news is strong. Each has a headline, then two or three paragraphs (parties, price or loan terms, size, location, who said what, what happens next), "Why it matters" in 2 to 3 sentences, "Interview angle" in one sentence, "Possible follow-up" as one question, and one source line with the outlet, date, and link.
5. "Also this week:" one paragraph on a smaller item worth knowing, with a source link.

## Who writes the briefings
Claude writes every briefing from sources it opens itself. Do not hand the writing to another tool. Each week, match the depth of the current briefing: no section may be thinner than this spec.

## Rules
- Stories must be published within the past 7 days. Market data may be older but must show its publish date.
- Every figure must come from a source. Never estimate. Undisclosed figures stay undisclosed.
- Links: one clean URL per source, with no tracking parameters (no utm_source).
- Sources: prioritize free, public outlets (Bisnow, The Real Deal, GlobeSt, Connect CRE, Commercial Observer, Commercial Property Executive, CBRE, Trepp, CREFC, NMHC, CoStar public releases, the Federal Reserve, and the Treasury). List any source you could not read at the end.
- Plain, direct language. No filler, no em dashes.

## Verify before sending
Re-open the source for each of these and confirm it appears in the article. Remove anything you cannot confirm and say so:
- Every figure in the Capital Markets table and in each asset class overview and market data paragraph
- Every Market Summary figure, matched exactly to its source
- Any claim about a filing or official document, checked against the filing itself
