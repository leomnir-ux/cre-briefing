# Weekly CRE Briefing Spec

## Purpose
A weekly reference for a student recruiting in CRE. I read the Market Summary every week, then pick the asset class sections I need (for example, only Industrial before a Prologis interview). Each section must make sense on its own without the others.

## Title
"U.S. CRE Weekly Briefing, [start date] to [end date]"

## Sections, in this order, with these exact headings
## Market Summary
## Capital Markets
## Office
## Retail
## Industrial
## Multifamily
## Hospitality
## Data Centers
## Other (Senior Housing, Life Sciences, and anything else notable)

## Market Summary (500 to 700 words)
Connected paragraphs covering: the biggest stories of the week, capital markets (rates, lending, spreads, cap rates, transaction volume, CMBS), and which asset classes are strengthening or weakening and why. Weave in interpretation, but start every interpretive sentence with "My read:". Put a link right after every sentence with a specific figure. A sentence that draws on more than one source gets a link for each source. Never let one "My read" sentence cover two asset classes. This section must work as a standalone briefing.

## Capital Markets
Rates, lending spreads, cap rates, CMBS delinquency and issuance, transaction volume, and available capital. Use a table for the indicators, with what each figure measures. End with a short "My read:" paragraph on what the measures mean together.

## Each asset class section, in this order
1. Section summary: 3 to 4 sentences on the state of this asset class this week.
2. Capital markets read-through: 1 to 2 sentences on how current rates, spreads, and lending conditions affect this asset class.
3. Market data: a short paragraph with the latest figures (vacancy, absorption, rents, delinquency). Show the publish date of each data source.
4. Stories: 2 stories, up to 3 only if the news is strong. Fewer is fine. Each story has a headline, a 4 to 6 sentence summary (parties, price or loan terms, size, location), "Why it matters" in 2 to 3 sentences, "Interview angle" in one sentence, "Possible follow-up" as one question, and one source line with the outlet, date, and link.

## Rules
- Stories must be published within the past 7 days. Market data may be older but must show its publish date.
- Every figure must come from a source. Never estimate. Undisclosed figures stay undisclosed.
- Links: one clean URL per source, with no tracking parameters (no utm_source).
- Sources: prioritize free, public outlets (Bisnow, The Real Deal, GlobeSt, Connect CRE, Commercial Observer, Commercial Property Executive, CBRE, Trepp, CREFC, NMHC, CoStar public releases, the Federal Reserve, and the Treasury). List any source you could not read at the end.
- Plain, direct language. No filler, no em dashes.

## Verify before sending
Re-open the source for each of these and confirm it appears in the article. Remove anything you cannot confirm and say so:
- Every figure in the Capital Markets table
- Every Market Summary figure
- Any claim about a filing or official document, checked against the filing itself
